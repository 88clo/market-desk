            Method and limits

            What an outlook means

            Each outlook is a two-year view, to about October 2028, measured against cash at roughly 4%. Positive means a return clearly ahead of cash with acceptable risk; Neutral means roughly cash-like; Negative means likely to lag cash. “Lean” marks a Neutral that would move first.

            The outlooks are research opinions and are not sharp enough to set position sizes on their own. The model mini-portfolio combines them with valuation and measured risk as one illustration; it is not a recommendation.

            How the three-month view is built

            Events are chosen because they could change a two-year outlook, not because they will move prices that week. Each has a most likely outcome and one alternative. Odds come from futures, prediction markets or polls where they exist and are otherwise labelled as judgement.

            “Improves” and “worsens” mean the fund’s two-year outlook would shift, in line with its tripwires. Many most-likely outcomes change nothing; that is the point of knowing them in advance.

            Two-year ranges

            The middle figure is the expected two-year total return implied by the outlook: cash at 4% a year plus a premium for a Positive rating (or a deduction for a Negative one), adjusted for valuation; bond funds use their yield. The range adds and subtracts one typical two-year swing, measured from the fund’s last five years of monthly prices. Roughly one time in six the result would be better than the top figure, and one time in six worse than the bottom. These are estimates built from opinions, not forecasts.

            The ▲ and ▼ lines name the single most important force that could push the fund toward each end of its range.

            The model mini-portfolio

            Expected returns are assumptions derived from the outlooks, not forecasts. Risk is measured from monthly prices since November 2016 (DBMF from mid-2019). Backtests show how a mix behaved, not how it will; the last five years favoured inflation hedges.

            Tripwires

            Each fund has one condition that would improve its outlook by a step and one that would worsen it. Thresholds are judgement, not statistics. Some upgrade conditions are the expected outcome, such as higher 2027 AI spending, and will be reset once they resolve.

            One outlook per fund

            A fund that appears in several frameworks carries one outlook; the reasoning changes with the angle. IEUR is the exception: the workbook uses it for five European sub-regions, which are rated separately.

            Editorial calls: property (VNQ, XLRE) leans negative in both tabs; broad emerging markets (IEMG) leans negative, not Negative, because Korea, China and India are each Neutral; semiconductors (SMH) are Positive at low conviction because the research disagrees on whether the chip cycle is peaking.
