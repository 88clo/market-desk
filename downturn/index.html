Dots mark each low. 2007 was down only 7% a month in; its low came 17 months later. 2000 took 30 months. Today's first weeks look like all of them, so the plan is set by stage, not by forecast.

What protected, peak to low total return, %

Only T-bills rose in all six. Long Treasuries paid when the Fed cut (2000, 2008, 2020) and lost more than stocks when it hiked (2022). Energy and commodities won only the inflation shocks.

Five lessons

  1. Cash-like bills are the only constant. Every other asset, gold included, lost money in at least one decline.
  2. Bonds hedge only when the Fed cuts. Today it is hiking, so keep bonds short-dated.
  3. Concentration is the risk in a valuation bust. 2000–02: Nasdaq-100 −83%, staples +24%. The top ten stocks are 37% of the S&P 500 (27% in 2000).
  4. Protection changes hands. Early winners give it back; the year after the low rewards what fell most (small caps +85% after 2009).
  5. Horizon decides the damage. 7 years to recover after 2000, 5½ after 2007, months after the last three. The S&P rose 9.5% the day after its 2025 low.

What history says to leaders

  1. Policy room sets the depth. In 2008 the Fed cut 4.5 points and in 2020 Congress added $2.2tn. Today the Fed is fighting inflation and the deficit is already 5.8% of GDP: assume a slower rescue.
  2. Chokepoints move prices fast. Hormuz took CPI from 2.4% to 4.2% in four months. Energy security is macro policy.
  3. Crises hide where leverage is opaque. 2007 began off balance sheet; today's equivalent is private credit and debt-financed AI capex.
  4. "Safe" depends on the Fed. Long bonds protected in three declines and failed in 2022, when inflation was the problem.

How to prepare an organization

  1. Liquidity runway first. Hold enough cash and committed lines to run 18–24 months without new funding. In 2008 and 2020 credit shut first.
  2. Refinance before you must. Extend maturities while markets are open; yields are at two-decade highs and could rise.
  3. Stress-test two shapes. A 2022-style shock (stocks and bonds both down, costs up) and a 2008-style freeze (revenue and credit down together).
  4. Pre-commit by trigger. Write the actions for each stage now; recoveries reward those who invest at the low instead of cutting.

1 · Where we are

An 11.6% correction, led by AI and chip stocks

Why it is falling

  • The AI unwind deepened. The S&P 500 lost 8.6% in the week to 20 November and Nvidia 23%. Selling hit chipmakers worldwide; Japan's and Korea's markets fell more than 5% on Friday.
  • The Fed is still tightening. October minutes showed nearly all officials expect a December hike and two floated half a point. Futures price about a 96% chance.
  • Lenders turned cautious. Global corporate credit spreads reached a 17-month high and banks grew wary of AI-related debt. US junk spreads, at 3.47%, are still calm.

5 · Positioning for the next few years

Match each pot of money to when you need it

The biggest decision is not which asset wins next month but how much is exposed to a long slump. History and today's prices point the same way: safe money now pays well, and expensive stocks have usually returned less over the following decade.

What that means. A Treasury bond that guarantees 2.9% above inflation now out-yields the stock market's cyclically adjusted earnings yield. That last happened around 2000. Valuation says little about the next one to five years, but over 10–16 years high starting valuations have reliably meant lower returns: a CFA Institute regression puts the S&P 500 near 2% a year from here. Stocks still belong in long-term money; the case for paying any price for them is weaker than usual.

7 · The plan

Decide now what we do at each stage

Nobody knows whether this pullback stops here or becomes 2000. A plan by stage means we never have to guess. We are now in the second column.

−5% to −10%

Pullback: prepare

  • Move 2–3 years of planned spending and the emergency fund into T-bills or a Treasury money fund.
  • Rebalance to our target stock share. Trim the biggest winners, especially AI and chips.
  • Write down the rules in the next three columns.

Spring 2026 stopped at −9% and set a record 16 days after the low.

−10% to −20%We are here

Correction: hold

  • No selling. Rebalance only if stocks drift 5 points below target.
  • Harvest tax losses in taxable accounts, swapping into a similar fund.
  • Don't buy hedges that have already run, such as energy.

2025 fell 19% and was back at a record 80 days after the low.

−20% to −35%

Bear market: rebalance in

  • Move money from the cash reserve and bonds back into stocks, per the rule.
  • Check jobs and credit. If unemployment and the junk spread are rising, expect a long one.
  • Keep spending from the reserve, never from stocks.

2022 fell 25% and took two years to recover.

Below −35%

Crash: stay the course

  • A second rebalance into stocks.
  • Long Treasuries pay off only if the Fed is cutting by then.
  • The reserve buys time: 2000 and 2008 took 5–7 years to recover.

2000 fell 49%, 2008 57%, 2020 34%.
