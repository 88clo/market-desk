    Every opportunity, most overlooked first

    Before buying: a quick checklist
    • Core first. A broad, low-cost index fund does most of the work. Treat themes as small add-ons.
    • Fee. The expense ratio is charged every year, so 0.6% versus 0.1% adds up over a decade.
    • Size and trading. Small funds (under about $100M) can have wide bid-ask spreads and sometimes close. Use limit orders.
    • Concentration. Check the top holdings. Some theme funds put 40% in two stocks.
    • What's priced in. A fund that has doubled already reflects the story. Out-of-favor themes have more room if the case plays out, and more risk if it doesn't.
    • Country funds. Single-country ETFs add currency and political risk on top of market risk.

    Patterns that repeat

    Click a year to jump there on the timeline.

    Openings after a political shift reward early movers

    Meiji Japan , China's reforms and Southern Tour , Vietnam's Đổi Mới , India's liberalization . In each case, the best returns went to those who arrived after the political signal and before the rules were fully set. Today: Vietnam's push for 10% growth and the reforms in the Gulf.

    Infrastructure booms overbuild, but the infrastructure lasts

    Britain's railway mania , the overbuilt US railroads of the 1870s , the telecom and fiber bust . Suppliers and later users often earned more than the original builders. Today: AI data centers. The bottlenecks, such as power and equipment, may be the safer side of the trade.

    Globalization waves reverse

    The first globalization ended in and only fully recovered decades later. The second has been fragmenting since the US–China trade war . When trade closes up, redundancy, local production and trusted suppliers become valuable. Today: China-plus-one and the processing of critical minerals.

    When the leading power changes, so does the financial system

    Britain's free-trade and gold-standard order gave way to the US-led Bretton Woods system , which ended with the Nixon shock . Today: US–China rivalry, sanctions and industrial policy are again redrawing where capital flows.

    Commodity booms end in busts

    The oil shocks were followed by the 1980s glut. The China-driven commodity supercycle ended in . Today: mineral prices may be volatile, so own processing capacity and contracted supply rather than betting on price.

    Expensive money rewards cash today

    After the Volcker shock , cash-generating businesses and buyouts outperformed. Today: with capital more expensive again, buying and modernizing traditional businesses can beat funding long cash burns.

    How to read this, and its limits
    • Regime types are simplified to nine categories. Many periods are mixed or disputed, for example Imperial Germany's elected Reichstag, Mexico under the PRI, or Turkey after 2016. Hover over a bar to see the exact label.
    • Leaders are listed in full since 1945. Before 1945, only the principal rulers are shown, so the leader row has gaps. The leader shown is whoever held effective power: the president in France's Fifth Republic, the party chief in communist states, and the de facto ruler where this differs from the formal title. Years are rounded, so changes within a year (Liz Truss in 2022, Andy Burnham replacing Keir Starmer in July 2026) share a slot.
    • Profiles and stability ratings cover leaders in office since 2008 and are judgment calls based on events up to November 2026, including the US midterms, Brazil's October election and the UK change of prime minister.
    • Countries were chosen as anchors for their regions, not as a complete list. Germany from 1949 to 1990 shows West Germany.
    • The forward-looking figures (AI capital spending, grid investment, FDI) come from UNCTAD's World Investment Report 2026 and UBS's outlook. The links between trends and opportunities, the crowding ratings, the historical analogs and the illustrative portfolio are judgment calls, not financial advice.
